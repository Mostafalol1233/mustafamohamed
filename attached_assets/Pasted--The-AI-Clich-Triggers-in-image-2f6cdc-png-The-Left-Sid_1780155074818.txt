🔴 The "AI Cliché" Triggers in image_2f6cdc.png
The Left-Side "Identity Block":
Having [Logo Icon] Name | Title separated by that thin vertical line (|) is the textbook AI design pattern. It's too crowded. The word "Developer" in gray looks faded and doesn't tell me anything exciting.

The "Sliding/Pill" Active State:
The light gray rounded background container behind "Skills" is incredibly overused. Every standard component library uses this exact soft-rectangle toggle effect for navigation items.

The Right-Side "Button Dumping Ground":
Shoving Twitter/X, YouTube, Email, Language, and Dark Mode into identical circular border buttons all in a row is lazy UX. It looks cluttered, and the spacing makes it feel like a standard boilerplate header rather than an intentional design choice.

🛠️ The Upgrades (How to make it look Premium & Human-Made)
Since you have that awesome Terminal/Console theme going on in your site, your navbar should feel like a natural extension of that dev-focused aesthetic, rather than a generic SaaS website header.

1. Fix the Left Side (Clean Identity)
What AI did: [M] Mustafa Mohamed | Developer

The Fix: Drop the vertical separator and the generic "Developer" subtitle entirely. Keep just your name or a clean text-based logo.

The Tech Touch: Make your logo look like a small piece of code. For example: mustafa.dev() or mmohamed ~/. This immediately sets a technical tone.

2. Redesign the Active State (Ditch the Pill)
Get rid of that rounded background capsule on the active link (Skills). Instead, use micro-interactions that feel like a terminal or code editor:

The Terminal Cursor Underline: Use a sharp, thin bottom line or a terminal cursor (_ or |) that appears next to or under the active text.

The Terminal Bracket: Wrap the active item in code brackets when active, like: [ Skills ] or <Skills/>. This requires almost no heavy CSS but shows huge intentionality.

3. Clean Up the Right-Side Icon Mess
You have 5 circles competing for attention. Let’s organize them logically:

Group the Socials: Move the X, YouTube, and Email icons out of the main navbar. A premium portfolio usually keeps social links fixed in a minimalist dock at the very bottom of the page, or strictly inside the Contact section.

Keep only Utilities: The only things that should remain on the top right are the Language Switcher and Dark Mode Toggle.

Style them better: Instead of putting them in thin gray circles, make them borderless icons that slightly change opacity or shift up by 1px or 2px on hover.
